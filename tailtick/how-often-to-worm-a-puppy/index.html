How often to worm a puppy

Puppies need worming much more often than adult dogs, because many are born with roundworm or pick it up from their mother's milk. The vet parasite experts at ESCCAP UK & Ireland advise starting at two weeks old, every two weeks until two weeks after weaning, then once a month until six months. After that, your vet sets a routine for an adult dog.

  • From 2 weeks old: worm every 2 weeks until 2 weeks after weaning.
  • Then: once a month until 6 months old.
  • After 6 months: at least every 3 months, monthly for some dogs. Your vet decides.
  • Always: a wormer made for puppies of that age and weight, and the gap the label gives.

This schedule comes from ESCCAP UK & Ireland, which writes parasite guidance for vets. It is aimed at roundworm (Toxocara canis), the worm puppies are most often born with. Other bodies give slightly different timings: the PDSA suggests starting at around three weeks and repeating every two to three weeks until 16 weeks, and says to check with your vet because each product has its own guidelines. Both agree that young puppies need frequent, regular worming.

This page does not give doses. The right product and amount depend on your puppy's age and weight, so follow the pack and your vet.

Why puppies need worming so often

The PDSA says many puppies catch roundworm in the womb or through their mother's milk, so they can have worms before they ever go outside. Roundworm rarely makes adult dogs seriously ill, but in puppies it can cause poor growth, a pot belly, diarrhoea and, in bad cases, a blocked gut.

There is a public health reason too. ESCCAP UK & Ireland says puppies and kittens are the biggest source of roundworm eggs in the environment, and those eggs can infect people. The PDSA notes young children are most at risk. Worming on time, and always picking up after your puppy, keeps eggs out of gardens and parks.

What to ask the breeder or rescue

  • Which wormer was used, and on which dates?
  • When is the next one due?
  • Was the mother wormed too?
  • Has the puppy had flea treatment, and with what?

Write the answers down and take them to your first vet visit. If you don't know when your puppy was last wormed, tell your vet rather than guessing. For everything else to sort out in the first week, see the new puppy checklist.

Choosing a puppy wormer

  • Check the age and weight on the pack. Many wormers are only for puppies above a certain age or weight.
  • Weigh your puppy first. Puppies grow fast, and the right amount changes with weight. Here is how to weigh your dog at home.
  • Prefer a product from your vet. The PDSA says prescription wormers are far more reliable than non-prescription ones, and advises against "off the shelf" products such as supermarket wormers.
  • Ask about lungworm. Not every wormer covers it. The PDSA calls lungworm a growing problem in the UK that can be serious for puppies as well as adult dogs.

After 6 months: adult worming

ESCCAP UK & Ireland's minimum for adult dogs is worming every three months, because it has been shown that less often makes no difference to how many roundworm eggs a dog passes. It suggests monthly worming for dogs that hunt or scavenge, eat raw food or offal, or live with young children or someone with a weak immune system. The PDSA says every one to three months is usually enough for most adult dogs, and more often for dogs that scavenge or hunt a lot.

Your vet can tell you which fits your dog's lifestyle, and whether a lungworm product is needed as well.

Fleas and tapeworm go together

The PDSA says dogs most often catch tapeworm by swallowing fleas while grooming, so flea control is part of worm control. See how often dogs and cats need flea treatment.

When to call your vet

  • Your puppy has a swollen belly, diarrhoea, vomiting, or is not growing or gaining weight.
  • You see worms in the poo, or segments like grains of rice around the bottom.
  • Your puppy seems unwell after a worming treatment.
  • You're not sure when your puppy was last wormed, or which product to use.

Getting a kitten instead? Kittens are wormed in a similar way but can start at three weeks: see the kitten first year checklist. For vaccines, see the puppy and kitten vaccination schedule.
