How often do dogs need vaccines?

Not every vaccine is yearly. Once the puppy course and first booster are done, some vaccines last for years and others need repeating every year. Here is how often adult dogs usually need each one, based on the World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) 2024 guidelines. Your vet sets the actual plan for your dog and your area.

  • Core vaccines (distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus): no more often than every 3 years in adults.
  • Leptospirosis: every year, where the disease is present.
  • Kennel cough (Bordetella, parainfluenza): usually every year, if your dog needs it.
  • Rabies: every 1 or 3 years, set by the product and local law.
  • Health check: once a year, even in years when no vaccine is due.

Why some vaccines last longer than others

The core vaccines are modified live vaccines. WSAVA says they should "not be given any more frequently than necessary" in adult dogs, and recommends core boosters every three years or less often. Most other vaccines, such as leptospirosis and kennel cough, protect for about a year, so they are repeated every year.

Some vaccine labels in some countries still say one year, even for core vaccines. WSAVA notes that its three-year advice can differ from the label, and that your vet will talk this through with you.

Core vs non-core vaccines

  • Core vaccines are for every dog, wherever they live: distemper, adenovirus and parvovirus. Rabies is core in countries where rabies is present, and leptospirosis where it is common.
  • Non-core vaccines depend on your dog's lifestyle and where you live: kennel cough for social dogs, Lyme disease where ticks carry it. The AAHA 2022 guidelines in the US also list canine influenza as lifestyle-based.

Before the adult schedule starts

Puppies have a course of core vaccines from 6 to 8 weeks, with the last dose at 16 weeks or older, then a booster at or after 26 weeks. After that, the adult intervals above apply. The RSPCA puts the first booster at 6 or 12 months. See the full puppy and kitten vaccination schedule, and the new puppy checklist for the first weeks at home.

Can a blood test replace a booster?

Sometimes. A titre test measures antibodies to the core diseases. WSAVA supports titre testing as a way to decide whether an adult dog needs its next core booster. It does not replace leptospirosis or kennel cough vaccines, and a low result does not always mean a dog is unprotected, so ask your vet what the result means for your dog.

Why the yearly check still matters

WSAVA encourages a health check about once a year, even when no vaccine is due. It is the time to review which vaccines your dog really needs, check weight and teeth, and agree on worming and flea treatment.

If a booster is late

Call your vet and tell them the date of the last vaccine. They will decide whether one booster is enough or your dog needs more, and whether your dog is still covered for boarding, daycare or travel. Kennels usually ask for an up-to-date vaccination card, so check the dates before you book.

Questions to ask your vet

  • Which vaccines does my dog need this year, and which can wait?
  • Is leptospirosis common here? Does my dog need the kennel cough vaccine?
  • Does my dog need rabies for travel, and how long does it last?
  • Is a titre test an option instead of the next core booster?
  • When is each vaccine next due? Write the dates on the card.
