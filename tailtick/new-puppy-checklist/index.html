New puppy checklist

Everything to sort out before and just after your puppy comes home: what to buy, how to make your home safe, how to get through the first day and night, and what to cover at the first vet visit. Tick it off as you go.

  • Before pick-up: buy the basics, puppy-proof the house, find a vet.
  • Day one: keep it calm, show the toilet spot first, keep the same food.
  • First week: a vet check-up, the next vaccines, worming and flea treatment booked.
  • Walks: not in public places until your vet says the vaccines have taken effect.

What to buy before your puppy comes home

The PDSA, the UK vet charity, suggests a shopping list like this:

ItemWhy
Crate and/or bed, blanketsA quiet, draught-free place to rest where nobody disturbs them.
Food and water bowlsWater should be easy to reach at all times, away from the bed.
Complete puppy foodLabelled for puppies, not adults. Start with the food they already eat.
Collar and ID tagIn the UK a dog must wear a collar and tag with your name and address in public.
Lead and harnessFor training at home now and walks later.
Car restraintFor the trip home and vet visits.
Toys, including chew toysKeeps them busy and away from your shoes and cables.
Puppy pads and poo bagsFor toilet training and, later, walks.
Brush or comb, dog toothpaste and toothbrush, dog shampooGet them used to being handled and groomed early.

Puppy-proof your home

  • Move cleaning products, medicines, cables and anything else they could chew well out of reach. Remember they grow fast, so check higher shelves and stairs too.
  • Use baby gates to keep them out of rooms you don't want them in, and away from stairs.
  • Check the garden: no gaps in fences, nowhere to dig under, and no poisonous plants.
  • Agree house rules with everyone at home before day one, such as sofa or no sofa. Puppies learn faster when everyone does the same thing.

Paperwork to ask for at pick-up

  • Vaccination record: which vaccines were given, on what date, and when the next one is due. The RSPCA says the previous owner should already have organised the first puppy vaccination.
  • Worming and flea treatment: what was given and when.
  • Microchip: in the UK every dog must be microchipped and registered by 8 weeks old, and GOV.UK says to ask for proof before you get the dog. Then update the database with your own details.
  • Food: what they eat, how much and when.
  • Something that smells of their mum and littermates, like a blanket, for their bed.

The first day

The PDSA's vets advise keeping the first day calm:

  • Take them to their toilet spot first, and praise them when they go there.
  • Show them their bed and bowls, then let them explore one or two rooms, not the whole house.
  • Keep to their usual meal times and food.
  • Offer toilet breaks often, especially after meals and naps.
  • Keep play short. Many puppies sleep 18 to 20 hours a day.
  • Skip visitors for a few days while they settle.

The first night

  • Pick a set bedtime and a fixed place to sleep.
  • For the first few nights, the PDSA suggests sleeping in the same room. Crying is normal, and it's fine to comfort them.
  • Put the blanket that smells of mum in their bed.
  • Expect a toilet break during the night for the first few weeks.

The first week

  • Vet check-up: register with a vet and book a check-up soon after your puppy arrives. Book the remaining vaccines and ask about worming and flea treatment.
  • Routine: meals, play, naps and bedtime at the same times every day.
  • Toilet training: reward going in the right place and never punish accidents.
  • Socialising: the RSPCA says 8 to 12 weeks is an important time. Carry your puppy outside to meet new people, sights and sounds, let them meet healthy, fully vaccinated dogs and cats, and get them used to the car, grooming and being handled.
  • Time alone: leave them alone for short periods to help prevent problems later.
  • Insurance and puppy classes: classes can fill up, so book early.

Questions for the first vet visit

  • When are the next vaccines due, and when can my puppy go out for walks?
  • Which worming and flea products suit my puppy, and how often?
  • Is the food right, and how much should they eat as they grow?
  • When should we talk about neutering?
  • Is the microchip working and registered to me?

When can a puppy go for walks?

Your puppy is unlikely to be fully vaccinated when you bring them home. The PDSA advises that until 1 to 2 weeks after the full first course of vaccines, you keep them away from places where unvaccinated dogs may have been. Carrying them outside is still a good way to socialise them. Ask your vet when it's safe for your puppy.

Once they can go out, start gently. The PDSA's general rule of thumb is about five minutes of exercise per month of age, twice a day, and less if your puppy is struggling.
