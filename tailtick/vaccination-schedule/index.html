Puppy and kitten vaccination schedule

A plain summary of when puppies and kittens usually get their core vaccines, based on the World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) 2024 guidelines. Your vet sets the actual plan for your pet and your area.

Puppies

AgeWhat usually happens
6 to 8 weeksFirst core vaccine: distemper, adenovirus (hepatitis) and parvovirus.
Every 2 to 4 weeks after thatRepeat the core vaccine. The last puppy dose is at 16 weeks or older.
From 12 weeksRabies, where it is required or the disease is present. A second dose follows a year later.
At or after 26 weeksCore booster. WSAVA advises this rather than waiting until 12 to 16 months.
AdultCore vaccines no more often than every 3 years. Rabies every 1 or 3 years, depending on the product and local law.

Leptospirosis, where it is common: two doses 2 to 4 weeks apart, then once a year.

Kittens

AgeWhat usually happens
6 to 8 weeksFirst core vaccine: feline panleukopenia, herpesvirus and calicivirus (often called FVRCP).
Every 2 to 4 weeks after thatRepeat it. The last kitten dose is at 16 weeks or older.
At or after 26 weeksCore booster.
AdultLow-risk cats: every 3 years or longer. Cats at higher risk (boarding, shows, many cats) may need the cat flu parts more often, up to once a year.

Rabies where required, and feline leukaemia (FeLV) for young cats where it is common, are decided with your vet.

Worming

Puppies and kittens need worming much more often than adults in their first months. The right interval depends on the product, your pet's age and weight, and local parasites, so follow your vet and the product label.
