How often do dogs and cats need flea treatment?

There is no single answer, because it depends on the product. Every flea treatment says on the pack how long one dose protects your pet. That number sets your schedule, and keeping to it without gaps is what stops fleas coming back.

  • How often: as often as the label says for that product, with no gaps.
  • All year? Often yes. Fleas can breed indoors all year in a heated home.
  • Who: every dog and cat in the house, not only the one scratching.
  • Cats: never use a dog flea product on a cat.

Do pets need flea treatment all year round?

Fleas are most common from late spring to early autumn, but a warm home keeps them going in winter too. ESCCAP UK & Ireland, the vet parasite experts, says fleas can develop indoors all year, and that infestations can appear out of season when the heating goes on, especially if owners have stopped treating. The RSPCA says you may need to treat all year if your home is centrally heated.

So stopping in autumn is a common reason fleas come back. Ask your vet what fits your pet and where you live.

Why gaps let fleas come back

Most of the problem is not on your pet. The RSPCA estimates that 95 per cent of flea eggs, larvae and pupae live in the home rather than on the animal. A female flea can lay up to 50 eggs a day, and ESCCAP UK says the life cycle can take as little as 12 to 14 days, or up to 350 days, depending on warmth and humidity.

That means new fleas keep hatching from carpets and bedding for weeks or months. If the treatment runs out in that time, they settle on your pet again.

Treat every pet in the house

ESCCAP UK advises treating all pets in the same household regularly, to stop them passing fleas back and forth. Use the right product for each species: dog products on dogs, cat products on cats, and ask a vet about rabbits and other small pets.

Indoor cats can get fleas too

People and other pets can carry fleas, eggs or larvae into the home without noticing. ESCCAP UK says even indoor cats should be checked for fleas so an infestation can be treated early. Ask your vet whether your indoor cat needs regular treatment or checks.

Never use a dog flea product on a cat

Some dog flea treatments contain permethrin, which is safe for dogs but very poisonous to cats. The PDSA says it is found in some dog spot-ons, shampoos, sprays and collars, and that poisoning most often happens when a dog product is put on a cat by mistake. Even a small amount can make a cat ill. If this happens, call your vet straight away.

How to check for fleas

  • Look for scratching, hair loss, red skin, spots or scabs, and tiny dark specks or small brown insects in the fur.
  • Comb your pet with a fine flea comb over white kitchen paper.
  • Drop a little water on the dark specks. If they turn reddish brown, it is very likely flea dirt.

Fleas can also pass on tapeworm if your pet swallows one while grooming. The RSPCA says that if your pet has fleas, make sure it is treated for worms too.

Treat the home as well

  • Vacuum floors, furniture, skirting boards, under furniture edges and where your pet sleeps. ESCCAP UK estimates vacuuming can remove up to half of flea eggs.
  • Empty or throw away the vacuum bag or dust after each use, as the RSPCA suggests.
  • Wash your pet's bedding regularly. Do not forget the car, the carrier and other places your pet spends time.
  • If you already have fleas, ask your vet about a home product that kills adult fleas and stops eggs and larvae developing.

If you missed a dose

Check the leaflet and ask your vet or pharmacist what to do. Do not give an extra dose to make up for the missed one unless they tell you to. Then restart the schedule from the day you treat, and set a reminder so the next one is not missed.
