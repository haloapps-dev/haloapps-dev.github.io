Kitten first year checklist

What happens in a kitten's first twelve months, in order: getting your home ready, the kitten vaccines, microchipping, neutering, the first trip outside and the first booster. Your vet sets the exact dates; this is the list to bring along.

  • 8 to 9 weeks: the usual age to come home. Keep the same food and routine.
  • Vaccines: a course of injections a few weeks apart, then a booster at around 6 to 12 months.
  • Microchip: in England, by 20 weeks old, even for indoor cats.
  • Neutering: UK cat charities advise four months, because kittens can get pregnant that young.

Get your home ready

The RSPCA's kitten care guide suggests having these in place before your kitten arrives:

  • A litter tray in a quiet spot that is easy to reach.
  • A scratching post, toys and things to climb.
  • Places to rest and to hide, and plenty of space to play.
  • A blanket from the kitten's first home, so their bed smells familiar.
  • A cat carrier, for the trip home and every vet visit.
  • The same kitten food they already eat.
  • A cat-proofed home with hazards out of reach.
  • A local vet, and pet insurance if you want it.

Paperwork to ask for

  • Vaccination record: which vaccines were given, on what date, and when the next is due.
  • Worming and flea treatment: the product and the date.
  • Microchip: the number and which database it is on, so you can put your own details on it.
  • Food: what they eat, how much and how often.

The RSPCA also warns that in the UK it is illegal for anyone other than the breeder to sell a kitten under six months old, and that it can be a sign of kitten farming.

Kitten vaccines

Kittens get a course of core vaccines against feline panleukopenia (enteritis), herpesvirus and calicivirus (cat flu), usually starting at 6 to 8 weeks and repeated every 2 to 4 weeks. The WSAVA 2024 guidelines advise the last kitten dose at 16 weeks or older and a booster at or after 26 weeks. For the full table, see the puppy and kitten vaccination schedule.

Microchipping

Since 10 June 2024, owned cats in England must be microchipped before they are 20 weeks old, with your contact details kept up to date on an approved database. This includes cats that stay indoors. Owners who don't comply can be fined up to £500. Rules differ in other countries, so check where you live.

Neutering at four months

Kittens can get pregnant from about four months old. The UK cat charities in the Cat-Kind group, including Cats Protection, the RSPCA and the PDSA, encourage neutering at four months. They say studies show neutering at this age does not raise surgical risks in the short term. Talk to your vet about timing for your kitten.

When can a kitten go outside?

Cats Protection recommends waiting until at least a week after the kitten vaccine course is complete, at around 14 weeks old, and making sure your cat is neutered, vaccinated and microchipped first. A new cat also needs about three to four weeks to settle into the home. For the first trip out, go just before a meal so they are more likely to come back in. Indoor cats can live happily too.

Questions for the first vet visit

  • When are the next vaccines, and when is the first booster?
  • Which worming and flea products suit my kitten, and how often?
  • When should we book neutering?
  • Is the microchip working and registered to me?
  • Is the food right, and when should we switch to adult food?
