How to keep your dog or cat's vaccination record

Boarding kennels, groomers, pet sitters, a new vet and border checks all ask for the same thing: proof of your pet's vaccines. A good record takes two minutes after each visit and saves a lot of searching later.

What to write down after every vaccine

Write downWhy it matters
Date givenEvery booster is counted from this date.
Vaccine name and what it coversFor example "DHPPi" for dogs or "FVRCP" for cats. Kennels check the diseases, not the brand.
Batch or lot numberIt is on the sticker your vet puts in the booklet. Ask for it if there is no sticker.
Clinic and vet nameA new vet or a kennel may call them to confirm.
Next due dateAsk your vet before you leave. Products differ: some last one year, some three.
Reactions, if anyTiredness, swelling or anything unusual, so your vet can plan the next dose.

Where to keep it

The paper booklet or pet passport is still the official record in most countries. Keep it with your pet's other papers, and take it to every visit so the vet can add the sticker.

A copy on your phone is what you will actually have with you. Take a photo of each new page after the visit. If the booklet gets lost, the clinic can usually print a history, but a photo is faster.

Your vet's records are the backup. If you move clinics, ask for the full vaccination history to be sent to the new one.

Before a kennel, groomer or trip

  • Check the next due dates at least two weeks ahead. Many kennels need some vaccines given a set time before the stay, so a last-minute booster may be too late.
  • Ask the kennel which vaccines they need. Kennel cough is often asked for even though it is not a core vaccine.
  • For travel abroad, rabies rules and waiting times depend on the country. Check the official rules for where you are going, and leave plenty of time.

When a booster is missed

Do not guess. Call your vet with the date of the last dose. Depending on how late it is and which vaccine it was, they may simply give the booster or restart the course.
