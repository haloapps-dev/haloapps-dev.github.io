How long should I walk my dog?

There is no single number that suits every dog. How much walking your dog needs depends on their age, breed, health and fitness. But UK vet charity PDSA and The Royal Kennel Club agree on a starting point: every dog should get out for at least one walk a day, and most need two. Here is how that changes from puppy to senior, and when to ask your vet.

  • Adult dogs: at least 1 to 2 walks a day. The PDSA says some breeds need at least one to two hours of exercise a day, and working breeds need much more.
  • Puppies: short, gentle walks only once fully vaccinated, with plenty of play and rest. No running or long hikes until fully grown.
  • Senior dogs: little and often. Shorter walks, but still out every day.
  • Unwell, injured or overweight dogs: follow your vet's advice.

What changes how much walking your dog needs

  • Breed. A working or gundog breed may need far more than a toy breed. The Royal Kennel Club suggests checking your breed's exercise needs.
  • Age. Growing puppies need their joints protected, adults need to stay fit, and seniors often need a bit less.
  • Health. A dog that is unwell or injured may need rest. The PDSA suggests puzzle feeders and snuffle mats to keep them busy while they recover.
  • Fitness. If you want your dog to start running or hiking with you, build up slowly to avoid injury.
  • Personality. Some dogs love a long brisk walk, others want to sniff. Both count: the PDSA notes that a slow, sniffy walk is good for their mental health.

Puppies: what about the 5 minute rule?

You may have heard the rule of five minutes of walking per month of age. The Royal Kennel Club gives it as a rule of thumb: five minutes of formal exercise per month of age, up to twice a day, until fully grown. That means 15 minutes (up to twice a day) at three months, 20 minutes at four months, and so on.

The PDSA is more cautious. It says there is no scientific evidence behind the 5 minute rule, and that it is not appropriate for most puppies. Both agree on the main point: puppies need less formal exercise than adult dogs, and too much high-impact exercise while they are growing can damage their joints. So treat any number as a ceiling, not a target:

  • Wait until your vet says your puppy is protected by their vaccines before walking in public places. See the puppy vaccination schedule.
  • Keep walks short and let your puppy rest whenever they're tired. Many puppies won't stop on their own, so pop them on the lead for a break.
  • Let them play freely at their own pace at home and in the garden, but don't take them running.
  • Avoid lots of jumping, twisting and skidding, and cover slippery floors at home.
  • Remember larger breeds grow for longer. The PDSA says most dogs are fully grown by one year, but breeds such as Labradors, Greyhounds, Dobermans and Great Danes can take 18 months to 2 years.

Senior dogs: keep going, gently

Older dogs tend to slow down and rest more, but the PDSA says they still need to get outdoors every day to sniff, stretch their legs and get some fresh air. Keep exercise regular and gentle, stick to fairly short and familiar routes, and watch the weather, as older dogs cope less well with heat and cold. If your dog starts struggling to get around or loses interest in walks, see your vet: conditions like arthritis can often be eased.

Is the garden enough?

Play in the garden is great, but the PDSA says play doesn't replace a good walk. Walks give your dog new smells, sights and other dogs to meet, which keeps their mind busy as well as their body. Training games, puzzle feeders and scent games are good extras, especially for busy breeds or on days when walks have to be shorter.

Walking safely

  • Hot weather: take water and walk in the cooler hours, early morning or evening. Heatstroke can be life threatening.
  • Dark evenings: a high-visibility coat, collar or harness helps drivers see your dog.
  • ID: in the UK your dog must wear a collar and tag with your name and address in public.
  • Recall: practise at home and in enclosed spaces before letting your dog off the lead, and keep them on the lead near roads and livestock.
  • After country walks: check your dog's coat for ticks, and keep flea and tick treatment up to date. Here is how often dogs need flea treatment.
