Boarding kennel checklist: how to choose kennels and what to pack

If friends, family or a pet sitter can't look after your pet, a good boarding kennel or cattery is the next option. Places vary a lot, and the change can be stressful for a pet, so it pays to check carefully and prepare early. This checklist covers what to look for when you visit, the vaccines kennels ask for, and what to take on drop-off day, based on advice from the RSPCA and the UK vet charity PDSA.

  • Book early. Good places fill up in summer and at Christmas.
  • Visit in person before you book, and check the licence.
  • Vaccines first: unvaccinated pets are turned away, and kennel cough often has to be given a few weeks before the stay.
  • Pack the vaccination record, enough usual food and medicine, written instructions, and something that smells of home.
  • Leave contacts: yours, a backup, and your vet.

Checklist: what to check when you visit

The RSPCA says to always visit a boarder before choosing one, and to ask your vet or friends for recommendations. Take this list with you:

CheckWhat to look for
LicenceAn up-to-date licence from the local council. In England the licence has a star rating, and the PDSA recommends looking for five stars, the highest.
InsuranceCover in case your pet needs emergency care during the stay.
FacilitiesClean, dry, draught-free and secure, with shade.
ExerciseWill your dog be walked or exercised every day, with space to behave normally?
Staff and contactEnough staff for the number of animals, and time spent with each pet every day.
MonitoringHow pets are checked during the day and in the evening.
SeparationPredator and prey species kept apart (for example dogs away from cats and rabbits).
Vaccination proofThey should ask to see it. A place that doesn't is a warning sign, because proof stops contagious diseases spreading.
Parasite treatmentDo they make sure all dogs and cats are up to date with flea and worm treatment?
Questions about your petA good boarder asks about diet and health, and tells you whether they can handle your pet's medical needs.
Vet emergenciesAsk what they do if your pet needs a vet and they can't reach you, and make sure that matches your wishes (the PDSA suggests checking this).
Overall feelDo you feel welcome, and do the animals look clean, happy and healthy?

If a boarder isn't happy to answer your questions, the RSPCA's advice is to keep looking until you find one you trust.

Vaccines: sort them out weeks before the stay

According to the PDSA, reputable kennels and catteries all want to know your pet's vaccinations are up to date, and unvaccinated pets won't be allowed to stay. Some places need a set gap between a vaccine and the start of the stay, so don't leave it to the last minute.

  • Kennel cough (dogs). The PDSA says most reputable kennels and daycare centres ask for the kennel cough vaccine two to three weeks before the stay. It lasts about 12 months, so it is repeated every year. It lowers the risk a lot but doesn't guarantee your dog won't catch it.
  • Core boosters. Check the dates on your pet's card. See how often dogs need vaccines and the adult cat vaccination schedule.
  • Ask the kennel exactly which vaccines they need and how long before arrival, then ask your vet to fit them in.
  • Flea and worm treatment should be up to date too. Here is how often dogs and cats need flea treatment.

What to pack for drop-off day

  • Your pet's vaccination record. The PDSA suggests taking it with you on drop-off (see how to keep a pet vaccination record).
  • More than enough of your pet's usual food for the whole stay. A sudden change of diet can upset their stomach.
  • Written feeding instructions: how much, and when.
  • Any medicines, with enough to cover the stay and written instructions: what to give, when (morning or evening, before or after food) and the easiest way to give it. Copy the vet's label; never change a dose.
  • A blanket or a worn piece of your clothing that smells of home, and a favourite toy.
  • Your contact details while away, plus a backup such as your hotel's name and phone number, a local emergency contact, and your vet's details.
  • How much exercise your dog usually gets (see how long to walk a dog), plus notes on fears or habits staff should know.

Is boarding right for your pet?

The PDSA notes that many pets find kennels and catteries quite stressful, and that cats especially are often better off staying somewhere familiar. Both the RSPCA and PDSA say care at home, by friends, family or a pet sitter, is usually the best option where you can arrange it. If you go that way, use our pet sitter care sheet. "Home from home" boarding, where your pet stays in someone's house, can suit nervous pets or those who need a little extra care.

This page is general information, not veterinary advice. Ask your vet which vaccines your pet needs and when, and always follow the medicine label. Sources: RSPCA, Pet sitting options (choosing an animal boarder); PDSA, Pet boarding, kennels and holiday care; PDSA, Kennel cough.
