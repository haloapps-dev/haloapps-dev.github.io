Territory privacy policy

Effective date: 9 November 2026 (updated the same day: map tiles and servers)

In short: Territory plans walks from where you are, so it uses your location while the app is open. Your walk history and your fog map stay on your phone. To draw the map and find streets, the app asks map services for the area around you. If you rate a place or share a route, that is stored under a random ID with no name or email. Crash reports are on unless you turn them off; anonymous usage statistics are sent only if you say yes. There are no ads.

Your location

Territory asks for your location ("while using the app") to plan a walk from where you are and to guide you along it. It does not track you in the background and does not keep a history of where you have been, except the walks you finish, which stay on your phone (see below).

To work, the app sends the area around you, not your identity, to these services:

  • OpenStreetMap Overpass (overpass-api.de, and when it is busy the public mirrors overpass.private.coffee, maps.mail.ru and overpass.kumi.systems): the centre and radius of the area to plan in (about 1 to 2.5 km). The centre is rounded to a grid of about 200 m, so it is not your exact position. The answer is kept on your phone for up to 30 days. Streets for Battersea and Clapham in London come with the app, so walks there need no download.
  • OpenStreetMap tiles (tile.openstreetmap.org): map images for the part of the map on screen.
  • Transport for London (api.tfl.gov.uk): when you choose to come back by bus in London, the point where your walk ended, to show nearby stops and the next buses.
  • Google Maps: only if you tap "Directions back to the start", which opens Google Maps with that start and end.

These services receive your device's IP address, as any website does, under their own privacy policies.

What stays on your phone

  • Your finished walks: when, how long, how far, the place you reached, and the line you walked (that is what clears the fog).
  • Which streets you have walked, your fog percentage and your settings.

We never receive this. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

Ratings and shared routes

Territory signs each installation in anonymously (Firebase Authentication) with a random ID. It has no name, email or phone number. Under that ID, our database (Firebase Realtime Database, hosted in Belgium, europe-west1) stores:

  • When you rate a walk: that you walked to that place or along that shared route, and when. Only walks you walked most of can be rated. Walks you don't rate are never sent.
  • Your rating of that place or route (loved it or not), and the running totals other walkers see.
  • If you tap Share this route: the route's line, its length, the place it leads to, and the walker name you choose. Other walkers nearby can then get your route. A shared route starts where your walk started, so don't share one that starts at your front door if you don't want people to know where that is.
  • If you report a shared route (the flag on its card): which route, the reason you picked and the time. We use reports to remove routes and walker names that break the rules. Hiding a walker's routes stays on your phone.

Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If the app crashes or hits a serious error it sends a crash report: the error, the app version, device model, operating system, and a random installation identifier. Never your location or walks. Turn it off in Settings › Send crash reports. Kept for 90 days.

Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase), only with your yes

If you tick Help improve Territory on the first screen, or later in Settings, the app sends anonymous events such as "a route was planned", "a walk was finished" or "a route was rated", with counts like minutes and kilometres. Never coordinates, place or street names, or your walker name. Until you answer, nothing is sent. Advertising features are off: no advertising ID, no ad personalisation. Kept for at most 14 months.

Notifications

If you allow it, Territory schedules one reminder on your phone after a week without a walk. Nothing is sent to set it up.

What the app does not do

  • No ads or advertising SDKs, no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No background location, contacts, camera or microphone.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data.

Service providers

Google LLC (Firebase: authentication, database, crash reports and statistics) processes data on our behalf, encrypted in transit (Firebase privacy information). The map services above are independent providers.

Retention and deletion

Data on your phone is deleted when you uninstall the app. To delete your ratings and shared routes from our database, email haloapps.dev@gmail.com with your walker name or roughly when and where you shared a route; we delete them within 30 days.

Delete your Territory data

Territory has no account, so deleting your data takes one email:

  1. Email haloapps.dev@gmail.com with the subject "Delete my Territory data".
  2. Tell us your walker name (Settings › Sharing in the app), if you set one, and roughly when and where you rated walks or shared routes, so we can find them.
  3. Within 30 days we delete your ratings, the "walked it" marks that go with them and your shared routes, and reply to confirm.
  4. To delete what is on your phone (your walks, your map and settings), uninstall Territory, or open Android Settings › Apps › Territory › Storage › Clear storage.

Crash reports and usage statistics are not linked to a name or email and expire on Firebase's standard schedule. You can stop them any time in the app's Settings › Privacy.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR or UK GDPR), you may have rights to access, delete or object to the processing of personal data. Our legal basis is providing the service you ask for (planning walks, ratings and sharing), our legitimate interest in keeping the app working (crash reports, which you can turn off) and your consent for usage statistics (which you can withdraw).

Walk safely

Routes come from OpenStreetMap data, which can be out of date. Watch the road, follow signs and local rules, and trust your own judgement about where to walk, especially after dark.

Children

Territory is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If a future version changes what is sent, we will update this policy before release.
