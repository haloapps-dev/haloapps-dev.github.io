Halo Reader privacy policy

Effective date: 11 November 2026

In short: your books, reading progress, highlights, notes and statistics are stored on your device. If you turn on sync, they are copied to a cloud storage account you connect (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive or your own WebDAV server) and go straight from your device to that service. Halo Apps has no servers that receive your books or your reading data, and we never see them. The app has no accounts of its own, no ads, and no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising SDKs.

What stays on your device

  • Books you import, their covers and details.
  • Reading data: your place in each book, bookmarks, highlights, notes and reading-time statistics.
  • Settings such as fonts, page colours and the name of this device.

Books are opened only by the app on your device. To show a book, the app runs a small web server that only your own device can reach (127.0.0.1); nothing is served to the network.

Sync with your own cloud (optional, off until you connect)

Sync is off until you connect a storage account in Settings › Sync. When it is on, the app copies to that account:

  • a small file per book with its title, author, your place in it, bookmarks, highlights, notes and reading time per device, plus the name you gave each device;
  • the book files themselves, unless you turn off Book files in the sync settings.

The files go directly between your device and the service you chose, over HTTPS. We are not in the middle and receive no copy. The service's own privacy policy applies to what it stores.

  • Google Drive: the app asks only for access to its own hidden app-data folder in your Drive (the drive.appdata permission). It cannot see, open or change any of your other Drive files. It also reads your Google account's email address to show which account is connected.
  • Dropbox: the app is limited to its own folder, Apps/Halo Reader. It cannot see the rest of your Dropbox. It reads your account's email address to show which account is connected.
  • OneDrive: the app asks only for its own app folder (Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder), shown as Apps/Halo Reader, and your basic profile (email address) to show which account is connected.
  • WebDAV: files go to the server address you enter, with the user name and password you enter.

Sign-in happens on the provider's own page; the app never sees your Google, Dropbox or Microsoft password. The access tokens it receives, and any WebDAV password, are kept in your device's secure storage (Keychain on iPhone and iPad, Android Keystore on Android) and are never sent to us. Turn off sync in Settings › Sync deletes them from your device. You can also remove the app's access at any time from your Google, Dropbox or Microsoft account settings, and delete the app's folder there.

Google user data: Halo Reader's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from your Google account is used only to store and read the app's sync files in your own Drive. It is not transferred to anyone, not used for advertising, and not read by people.

Things you choose to send

  • Look up and translate: when you select text and tap Look up or Translate, the app opens your browser on a Google search or Google Translate page with that text. On iPhone and iPad, the system dictionary is used when it has the word, on your device.
  • Links in a book open in your browser when you tap them.
  • Share and export: books, quotes and highlight exports go to the app or place you pick in the share sheet. That app's own privacy policy then applies.
  • Read aloud uses your device's text-to-speech engine. Some engines offer voices that work over the network, under the engine provider's terms; the app sends nothing to us.

What the app does not do

  • No Halo Apps account or sign-up.
  • No advertising, no advertising ID, no analytics, crash-reporting or attribution SDKs, no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone or photo library.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state (Android): used only for sync with the account you connect, and to wait for Wi-Fi when you choose "book files on Wi-Fi only".
  • Files: the app opens only the files you pick in the system file picker or send to it with "Open with" or "Share".
  • Background audio (iPhone and iPad): lets read aloud continue when you lock the screen.

Data retention and deletion

Your books and reading data stay on your device until you delete them in the app or uninstall it; uninstalling removes all of the app's data from that device. On Android the app opts out of automatic cloud backups. On iPhone and iPad your device's own backup (iCloud or a computer) may include the app's data, under Apple's terms.

Copies in your cloud account stay there until you delete them. Deleting a book while sync is on removes it from your connected account and your other devices too. Because we hold none of your data, there is nothing for us to delete; if you have a question, email us.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR or UK GDPR), you may have rights to access, correct, delete or object to the processing of personal data. Halo Apps does not collect or hold personal data from Halo Reader, so these rights are exercised directly: in the app, and with the cloud provider you connected.

Children

Halo Reader is suitable for all ages and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone.

Changes

If a future version changes what is sent or to whom, we will update this policy before release. Any new network feature will be off by default and clearly labelled.
