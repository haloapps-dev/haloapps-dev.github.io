Delete similar photos on Android and keep the best one

Bursts, group shots taken five times, the same sunset from three angles: similar photos are often the biggest pile in a gallery, and the hardest to clear, because you have to decide which one to keep. Exact-duplicate finders don't catch them, because the files are not identical. Here is how to find them on Android, how to choose the best shot quickly, and how to delete the rest safely.

Google Photos: use photo stacks

Google Photos describes similar photos as nearly identical photos of the same subject taken within a short time. When Stack similar photos is on, it shows them as one stack and chooses a Top pick as the cover. Stacks work with backed-up photos.

  1. Open Google Photos. To check the setting, tap More and make sure Stack similar photos is on.
  2. In the Photos view, tap a photo with the stack icon at the top right. Scroll the filmstrip at the bottom to compare the shots.
  3. If the top pick is not the one you like best, go to your favourite, tap the three-dot Thumbnail menu and tap Set as top pick.
  4. To clear the rest, use Keep this, delete rest: it keeps the photo you are looking at and moves the others in the stack to the Trash.
  5. To delete only some of them, tap the Thumbnail menu, then Multi-select, select the photos, and tap Trash > Move to trash.

Be careful with Trash on a whole stack: touching and holding a stack and tapping Trash deletes the entire stack, top pick included. Source: Google Photos Help: Organize your Photos view. Deleted photos go to Collections > Trash, and deleting a backed-up photo also removes it from your Google account.

Samsung Gallery: open grouped similar images

On newer Galaxy phones, Samsung Gallery can show similar images under one thumbnail with a small number (2, 3 and so on) on it.

  1. Open Gallery. If you don't see grouped thumbnails, tap More options (three dots) and choose Group similar images. On some models it is a square icon at the top of the screen.
  2. Tap a thumbnail with a number to see all the shots in the group.
  3. Touch and hold the shots you don't want, tap Delete, then Move to Trash.

For exact copies, Samsung Gallery also has Menu > Clean out > Delete duplicate pictures, which keeps the originals. Source: Samsung: Use the Gallery app on your Galaxy phone or tablet. Deleted items stay in Menu > Trash for a while before they are removed.

Any phone: sort a run of shots by hand

If your gallery app doesn't group similar photos, you can still clear them quickly, because similar shots sit next to each other in your camera roll. Open the photos from one day, find a run of near-identical shots, and compare them full screen. Then select all but the best one and delete them. For exact copies, such as a photo saved twice, Files by Google can help: Menu > Clean, then Select files on the Duplicate files card, keep the file marked Original and move the copies to the Trash (Files by Google Help: Delete duplicate files). How to pick the best shot fast

The slow part is deciding. A simple order of checks makes it quicker:

  1. Sharp: zoom in on the main subject's face or eyes. Drop anything blurry or out of focus.
  2. Eyes open: in people shots, drop frames where someone is blinking or looking away.
  3. Expression: of what is left, keep the one where people look most like themselves.
  4. Framing: if it is still a tie, keep the straightest, best-framed shot. You can crop and edit it later.

Keep one, or two if they are really different. If you can't decide in a few seconds, keep both and move on: the time you save on the clear cases matters more.

Before you delete

  • Deleted photos usually go to a Trash or Recycle bin first, so you can bring back a mistake. See how the Trash and Recycle bin work on Android.
  • If you use Google Photos backup, deleting a photo in Google Photos removes it from your account too. To only remove the phone copy, read what Google Photos "Free up space" really deletes.
  • Some apps save edited copies or "motion" versions alongside the original. Check you are keeping the version you want before deleting the rest.

Common questions

What is the difference between duplicate and similar photos?

A duplicate is the same file saved twice, so you can delete the copy without looking. Similar photos are different shots of the same moment, such as a burst, so you have to choose which one to keep.

Why doesn't my duplicate finder show my burst photos?

Each frame in a burst is a different picture, so tools that look for exact copies don't flag them. Use a feature that groups similar photos, like Google Photos stacks or Samsung Gallery's grouped images, or compare the shots yourself.

Does deleting a Google Photos stack delete every photo in it?

Trashing a whole stack does. To keep one, open the stack and use Keep this, delete rest, or use Multi-select to choose exactly which photos to delete.
