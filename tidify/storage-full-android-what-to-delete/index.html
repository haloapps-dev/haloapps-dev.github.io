Android storage full? What to delete first

When your phone says storage is full, it is tempting to delete whatever you see first. A better way is to start with things you will never miss, like files already in a bin and old downloads, and only then move on to apps and photos. Here is a safe order to work through, with where to find each item on Android.

First, see what is using the space

  1. Open your phone's Settings app and tap Storage.
  2. Look at the categories, such as apps, images, videos and system. The biggest one is where to spend your time.
  3. On a Samsung Galaxy, go to Settings > Battery and device care > Storage (called Device care on some older versions).

The "System" part can't be cleaned by hand, so focus on the rest.

1. Empty the bins

Files you deleted often still use space. Google Photos, Files by Google and Samsung Gallery and My Files each keep deleted items in their own Trash or Recycle bin, usually for 30 days. Look through each one, then empty it.

  • Google Photos: tap Collections, then Trash.
  • Files by Google: tap Menu, then Trash.
  • Samsung: Settings > Battery and device care > Storage. Under Recycle bin, tap an app and tap Empty.

Once a bin is emptied, those files can't be brought back. If you are not sure, read how the Trash and Recycle bin work on Android first.

2. Clear old downloads

PDFs, installers, memes and videos saved from the web, email and chats pile up in the Downloads folder. In Files by Google or Samsung My Files, open Downloads, sort by size, and delete what you no longer need.

3. Remove offline media

Downloaded films, playlists and podcasts can be large. Google's advice is to delete them from the app you used to download them. In YouTube Music, for example: profile photo > Settings > Downloads & storage > Clear downloads. You can download them again when you have space.

4. Archive or uninstall apps you don't use

  1. Open Settings and tap Apps.
  2. Tap an app you no longer use.
  3. Tap Archive to remove the app but keep its data and icon (Android 15 and up, on supported phones), or Uninstall to remove it completely.

To use an archived app again, tap its faded icon and it reinstalls from the app store. On Samsung, Storage > Unused apps lists apps you haven't opened in a while.

5. Clear app cache (a short-term fix)

Apps keep temporary files called cache. Clearing it is safe for your data, usually under Settings > Apps > the app > Storage or Storage & cache > Clear cache. Google calls this a short-term solution: the cache builds up again over time. Don't tap Clear data or Clear storage unless you mean to: that resets the app and can delete downloads and settings kept inside it.

6. Delete big videos

A few long videos can take more space than hundreds of photos. Use a file manager that sorts by size, such as Files by Google (Menu > Clean > Delete large files) or Samsung's Storage > Large files, and play each video before deleting it. Full steps: how to find large videos taking up space on Android.

7. Clean out screenshots, duplicates and similar photos

Screenshots of tickets and codes, saved copies from chats and bursts of near-identical shots add up. Work through them in your gallery, or use Samsung's Storage > Duplicate files. See how to delete screenshots in bulk, how to delete duplicate photos on Android and how to clear WhatsApp media taking up space.

8. Remove phone copies of backed-up photos

If your photos are backed up with Google Photos, you can remove the copies on the phone and keep them in your account: tap your profile picture, then Free up space on this device. Recent items may stay on the phone. Don't use the Google Photos Trash for this: deleting a backed-up photo there removes it everywhere. Details: what Google Photos "Free up space" really deletes.

What not to delete

  • Files you don't recognise in system folders. Android and your apps need them. Stick to Downloads, media and apps you know.
  • Your only copy of a photo or video. Check it is backed up or copied to a computer before you remove it.
  • App data for chats and notes. "Clear data" can wipe history that is not backed up.

Common questions

Why is my Android storage still full after deleting photos?

They are probably in a Trash or Recycle bin, where they still use space until the bin is emptied or the items expire. Check every bin in step 1.

Is it safe to clear cache on Android?

Yes, clearing cache removes temporary files, not your photos or messages. An app may load a little slower the first time afterwards. Clearing data is different and resets the app.

Should I use a cleaner app?

Android's own Storage screen and Files by Google already cover bins, downloads, apps and large files. A photo cleaner helps most when photos and videos are the biggest category, because it lets you review them quickly. Pick one that keeps your photos on the phone and lets you review before anything is deleted.
