Clean up your camera roll on Android in 5 minutes a week

A big gallery clean-up takes hours, so most people put it off until the phone says storage is full. A short weekly habit is easier: you only look at the photos from the last seven days, while you still remember why you took them. Here is a five-minute routine, one minute per step, and where to find each item on Android.

Before you start: pick a time

Choose a moment you already have each week, like Sunday evening or the bus ride home on Friday, and add a repeating reminder in your calendar or clock app. Doing it at the same time each week is what makes it stick. The first session takes longer if your gallery is already full; from the second week on, you only have seven days of photos to look at.

Minute 1: delete this week's screenshots

Screenshots of tickets, codes, maps and chats are useful for a day and then just take space. They are easy to clear because Android keeps them in one folder.

  1. Open your gallery app (Google Photos, Samsung Gallery or your phone's own) and open the Screenshots album. In Google Photos it is under Collections; in Samsung Gallery under Albums.
  2. Touch and hold the first screenshot from this week, then tap the others you no longer need.
  3. Tap Delete and confirm. They go to the Trash or Recycle bin first.

Once a month, Files by Google can also sweep up older ones: Menu > Clean, then the Old screenshots card, which lists screenshots more than 4 weeks old (Files by Google Help: Delete your screenshots). For a big backlog, read how to delete screenshots on Android in bulk.

Minute 2: clear photos saved from chats

Memes, forwarded photos and stickers from group chats are often saved to your phone automatically. In your gallery, open the album named after the chat app, such as WhatsApp Images, and delete what you don't want to keep. To stop new ones piling up, you can turn off automatic saving in the chat app's settings. Full steps for WhatsApp: how to clear WhatsApp media taking up space.

Minute 3: keep the best shot, delete the rest

Most of us take three or four shots of the same moment. Scroll through this week's camera photos, and where you see a run of near-identical shots, keep the sharpest one and delete the others. Exact copies, such as a photo saved twice, can be found with Files by Google:

  1. Open Files by Google and tap Menu, then Clean.
  2. On the Duplicate files card, tap Select files.
  3. Keep the file marked Original, select the copies, and tap Move to Trash.

Source: Files by Google Help: Delete duplicate files. On Samsung, Settings > Battery and device care > Storage also shows Duplicate files. More detail: how to delete duplicate photos on Android.

Minute 4: check this week's videos

One long video can take more space than hundreds of photos. Look at the videos you recorded this week: trim or delete the ones you won't watch again, such as accidental recordings and long clips where you only need a few seconds. To find the biggest files on the whole phone, use Files by Google Menu > Clean > Delete large files (Files by Google Help: Delete large files) or Samsung Storage > Large files. Full steps: how to find large videos taking up space on Android.

Minute 5: check the Trash and your backup

Deleted photos usually stay in a Trash or Recycle bin for 30 days, so you can still bring back anything you removed by mistake.

  • Google Photos: tap Collections, then Trash. If a photo was backed up, deleting it here removes it from your Google account too (Google Photos Help: Delete photos and videos).
  • Samsung Gallery: tap Menu, then Recycle bin.
  • Files by Google: tap Menu, then Trash.

Take a quick look for anything you want back. You don't need to empty the bins every week, because items are removed automatically; empty them only when you need the space now. More on this: how the Trash and Recycle bin work on Android. If you use Google Photos backup, also check that this week's photos have finished backing up before you rely on it.

Once a month: a bigger check

  • Open Settings > Storage to see whether images, videos or apps are growing fastest.

Common questions

How often should I clean my camera roll?

Once a week works well for most people: it is often enough that each session is short, and the photos are recent enough that deciding is easy. If you take few photos, every two weeks is fine.

Is "camera roll" the same as the gallery on Android?

"Camera roll" is the iPhone name. On Android the same photos are in your gallery app, such as Google Photos or Samsung Gallery, and the photos you take yourself are usually in the Camera album.

Will deleting photos from my gallery delete them from my backup?

In Google Photos, yes: deleting a backed-up photo moves it to the Trash and removes it from your account too. If you only want to remove the copy on your phone, use Free up space on this device instead.
