Your serve, measured from one phone

Clip your phone behind the baseline, tap start and serve a basket. Courtella says each serve's speed and call out loud as it lands, then shows you what changed since last time. No radar gun, no helper, no account.

How it works

  1. Clip the phone behind the baseline. A fence or a stand is fine. About 2.3 m (7.5 ft) up sees the far service boxes best, and lower still works.
  2. Tap start. Courtella finds the court lines by itself. You can fix them by hand if the light is tricky.
  3. Serve. Each serve gets its estimated speed, in or out and where it landed, said out loud so your back can stay to the phone.
  4. Read your report. After every basket: fastest serve in, % in, first against second serves, the landing map, your toss and one or two things to work on.

What you get

  • Speed on every serve, with how sure it is. Each speed comes with a ± range. If the camera couldn't read one, you see a dash, never a made-up number.
  • In or out, and where. T, body or wide, deuce or ad box. Calls too close for the camera are marked as close, not guessed.
  • Practice with a purpose. First serves, second serves, targets called before each serve, and a 24-serve test to repeat month by month.
  • Your serve over time. Speed, % in, toss steadiness and second-serve kick, session by session, with your bests.
  • Share with your coach. A card of your session, your best serves back to back, or every serve as a spreadsheet.

Private by design

Tracking runs on the phone, so a court with no signal is fine. Video, sound and your sessions stay on your phone. No account and no ads. Anonymous usage stats are sent only if you turn them on.

Honest numbers

Radar guns and TV show the ball's speed just off the racket. Some camera apps show the average speed over the whole flight, which reads 12-25% lower for the same serve. Courtella estimates the speed off the racket and shows its range, so you can compare it with a radar gun. Read why serve speeds disagree.
